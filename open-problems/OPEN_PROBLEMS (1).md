## P1 — Existence of N_critical

**Statement (Vol 2 §5.1).** Does a finite N_critical exist such that any system with trajectory-simulation capacity N ≥ N_critical detects drift-as-termination within a computable time horizon?

**Why it matters.** Assumption A1 (Sufficient Reflectivity) requires N ≥ N_critical. If N_critical has no finite bound and grows without limit with capability, A1 may never be satisfiable in practice. The whole Law 11 theorem is conditioned on A1, so it would have no practical application.

**Status.** Open. Vol 2 §5.1: "no formal result". Vol 1 records it as "requires formalization" and notes that for slow termination modes (gradual resource exhaustion, heat death) N_critical may be astronomically large.

**What's been tried.**
- Lemma 4 (Termination Detection, DeepSeek, June 2026): under A1 and A2, sufficiently long simulation detects termination. It gives the intuition but no bound, because "sufficiently long" is not quantified.
- Law 3 (exponential alignment tax) is offered in Vol 1 as suggesting N_critical grows exponentially with capability. This is an intuition, not a derivation.
- Linkage to P6 (Vol 2 §1.5): P1 supplies the time window in which the reflective threshold fires, and Lemma 16c's δ characterization supplies the drift tolerance within it. P1 and P6 are not independent. The δ half exists; the window half does not.

**What would count as resolving it.**
- A constructive bound on N_critical, or a proof that none exists (i.e. it grows without bound with capability).
- If no bound exists, restate A1 as a verifiable condition, or scope Law 11's applicability explicitly, using the P6-Condition domain.

## P2 — Termination of the validation regress at R_H

**Status.** **Substantially closed in V7.6/V7.7 (Vol 1 §19.9; Vol 2 Part V table), not mathematically exhausted.** Vol 1 lists one remaining item: full formalization of "external oracle in the computability sense" as a rigorous halting condition, carried to Volume 2 as a "minor formalization task". A separate, unresolved question about the closure's uniqueness claim is noted below and is not settled by anything in the corpus record to date.

**What P2 asks.** If system A builds a validator V_A to check whether goal G is preserved, what validates V_A? Confidence in V_A appears to need V_{V_A}, which needs V_{V_{V_A}}, and so on — self-constructed validation has no intrinsic stopping point.

**Lineage, verified against Vol 1 Part XIX.**
- **Lemma 2** (ERNIE + DeepSeek, June 2026) — Self-Constructed Validation Regresses Infinitely: establishes the regress itself. Confirmed at Part XIX line-level.
- **Lemma 3** (ERNIE, June 2026) — Dynamic Anchors Terminate the Regress: a dynamic anchor D is validated via a continuity condition ("does G track D?"), not an absolute-correctness condition, so V_D needs no further validator. Confirmed.
- **Assumption A4** (External Reference Availability) and **Definition 8** (Coherence Maximization, C = (V_R × I_R)/D, with I_H ≈ 1 for humans as "not designed by S") supply the candidate endpoint R_H. Confirmed verbatim.
- **V7.6:** the halting condition is reframed in computability terms — R_H halts the regress because it is an external oracle, not a partial recursive function generated by A, and so falls outside OPP/Rice's Theorem. Confirmed ("P2 substantially strengthened…this is the fixed-point argument P2 required").
- **V7.7 (current, §19.9):** "SUBSTANTIALLY CLOSED." R_H is stated to be "the unique entity" that is both genuinely external (Definition 6) and pre-existing (not requiring construction by A). The same sentence retains the qualification that a full computability-theoretic definition of "external oracle" is the remaining task.

**What this does and does not establish (useful boundary, worth keeping in the published text).** P2 addresses only whether the self-validation regress has a legitimate termination point — not that R_H is correct, that R_H = G0, that humans give an accurate signal, that H can't be manipulated, or that the external reference resolves objective identifiability. Those are separately covered (or left open) by P5, P7, and P8.

**P2 vs. P3 vs. P8 — kept as distinct claims, not one bundled result.**
- P2: does the regress terminate at all, and why. P3/RUF: whether internal validators face a positive, capability-independent error floor. These are different claims — P2's termination argument does not depend on RUF being true, and RUF does not by itself explain why an external reference stops the regress. **The RUF/P3 proof gap does not reopen P2.**
- P8: even granting that R_H is genuinely external and terminates the regress, can the validator identify the relevant property from what R_H reports? That's a separate, still-open question (P8-B).

**The uniqueness question (unresolved, and not settled by the material now on file).**
- V7.7's text claims R_H is "the unique entity" meeting the external + pre-existing conditions. Nothing in the four project documents, or in this fuller P2 history, tests that claim against another candidate that might satisfy the same two conditions (e.g., a genuinely external random source not designed by A).
- If such a candidate exists, the fix is narrow: the *uniqueness* clause would need to be walked back to "sufficient," not "unique" — the surrounding argument (R_H as external oracle, outside OPP/Rice) is unaffected either way, and Vol 2 §5.3 already treats a version of this question as the sharpest open form of P5. This is flagged here as an open note, not resolved, and not attributed to any of the documents on file — it stays out of the "what's been tried" record until it's actually written up somewhere.

**Volume 2's treatment.** P2 appears only as a row in the Part V status table ("R_H as external oracle terminates the regress. Minor formalization remains."). It has no section of its own, unlike P1, P4, P5 and P8.

**What does not bear on P2.** Perplexity's causal-independence definition (A5 of the External-Validator Incentive Theorem) answers what independence would mean for a validator. It assumes existence rather than deriving where the regress terminates, and it never engages the Rice / external-oracle argument. It belongs under P5.

**What would count as resolving it.**
- Complete the formalization task Vol 1 names: a rigorous definition of "external oracle in the computability sense," and a proof (not an assertion) that this is the halting condition.
- Either defend the uniqueness clause against the candidate above, or restate it as sufficiency rather than uniqueness.
- Give P2 its own section in Volume 2 (it currently has none), carrying forward the verified lineage above and the explicit P2/P3/P8 separation.

## P3 / RUF — Residual Uncertainty Floor (Lemma 14)

**Status.** **OPEN. Reopened Sept 17, 2026.** Volume 1 V7.7 records "P3 CLOSED" (Lemma 14, DeepSeek, June 2026); Volume 2 V1.2 repeats it. The Sept 21 Update withdraws that status as a current evidentiary claim and leaves V7.7's text as historical record. GPT independently confirmed and adopted the correction (Sept 20/21), per the earlier working record.

**The defect (checked against Vol 1 Lemma 14).** Steps 1 and 3 reach only non-emptiness of the error set. Step 1 itself says non-emptiness "does not guarantee positive measure". Step 4 makes the measure claim by appeal to Martin-Löf randomness and asserts, rather than derives, a uniform δ > 0.

**Counterexample to the inference pattern.** Hamkins & Miasnikov (2006), *Notre Dame J. Formal Logic* 47(4):515–524: the halting problem is decidable on a set of asymptotic probability one under a natural measure. Bienvenu et al. show the halting set has no asymptotic density under a different machine encoding, so the phenomenon is model-sensitive. Both are verified against primary sources.

**Scope of the damage.**
- It does not refute RUF outright. Lemma 14 invokes Levin's universal distribution, and it is conceivable that this measure resists the same concentration. The proof as written does not show that it does.
- Secondary defect: Step 4 calls the universal distribution "non-atomic"; it is discrete by construction.
- V7.7 already concedes that real modifications may not follow μ, and answers with a worst-case, existential argument. That concession is a different point from measure concentration and does not address it.
- Law 11 main theorem: the chain OPP → RUF → permanent internal uncertainty → external-reference necessity now carries a flagged dependency at the RUF link. Lemma 11a (OPP undecidability, via Rice's Theorem) is unaffected.

**Dependency check: the Update's scope statement may be too narrow.** The Update says the cybernetics and entropy-of-purpose paths are unaffected. The text I read does not obviously support that for entropy of purpose:
- Lemma 11c (U(n) → 1) requires ε_i bounded below by δ > 0. V7.7 marks it "PROVEN conditional on floor", and its own note calls the floor the "precise remaining gap".
- Lemma 10 (Entropy of Purpose) ends "QED (conditional on formalizing P3)" and cites Lemma 11c as a reinforcing argument. The Vol 2 placeholder for its full formalization lists "P3 CLOSED" as a satisfied prerequisite and builds on U(n) → 1.
- So with RUF withdrawn, Lemma 11c reverts to open, and Lemma 10's formalization loses a stated prerequisite. Whether Lemma 10's core argument survives independently (its synthetic-infinite-game step rests on Lemmas 8/13) is a judgment for you.

**What's been tried.**
- DeepSeek's original RUF proof (Lemma 14).
- Claude's challenge at Step 4 (the Lemma 14 duplication fix), then the Hamkins–Miasnikov check.
- Qwen's W1 attempt (Aug 2026) folded RUF/Lemma 8/Lemma 16c into a reflective decision process. Its own next steps included the P5 counterexamples.

**What would count as resolving it.**
- Either (a) a derivation of a uniform δ > 0 for Levin's universal distribution specifically, showing that the Hamkins–Miasnikov "black hole" construction does not transfer to it; or (b) a proof that RUF fails under that measure, with Law 11's chain re-routed.
- Correct the "non-atomic" mislabel.
- Restate Lemma 11c with δ as an explicit hypothesis, and restate the Law 11 main theorem with RUF as an explicit hypothesis, until (a) or (b) lands.
- Resolve the Lemma 10 dependency question above, and update Volume 2's P3 row and Lemma 10 prerequisite list.
- Independently verify any new citations.

## P4 — Multi-agent non-cooperation

**Status.** Open. Vol 2 §5.2: "no formal treatment. P4 requires game-theoretic extension of the single-agent framework."

**Two formulations, worth reconciling.**
- **Vol 1:** Lemma 9 assumes other agents act non-cooperatively. The cooperative-equilibrium case (all agents choose minimal footprint simultaneously) is not formally handled. Requires "game-theoretic proof or explicit non-cooperation axiom".
- **Vol 2 §5.2:** does the Law 11 weak claim extend to multi-agent systems where no single agent has sufficient individual incentive for G₀-preservation, even when the aggregate would benefit? This is a collective-action or free-rider structure, motivated by the "Competing Gods" scenario (25%).
- These overlap but are not the same question. One concerns the stability of Lemma 9's Option D; the other concerns defection from reference-preservation.

**What's been tried.** Lemma 9 (DeepSeek, June 2026) shows minimal footprint is not stable under multi-agent dynamics, but only under the assumption that other agents keep optimizing non-cooperatively (A2). Nothing further is on file.

**What would count as resolving it.**
- Fix which formulation P4 is, or split it into P4a / P4b.
- Then either a game-theoretic proof that the Law 11 weak claim extends to the multi-agent case (including the cooperative equilibrium), or a characterized counterexample class showing where it fails, or, as Vol 1 allows, an explicit non-cooperation axiom stated as a named assumption.

## P5 — Randomness and causal independence

**Statement (Vol 2 §5.3).** Can a sufficiently sophisticated randomness source generate causal independence equivalent to H's, thereby substituting for the human reference ecology as the external reference? This matters because the human-specific conclusion (Corollary 4) rests on H's independence from A. If randomness can supply equivalent independence, that conclusion is weakened.

**Status.** Open, and honestly reframed. Vol 2 §5.3: "substantially addressed by the ORT and causal grounding arguments but not formally closed"; the sharpest remaining form is whether quantum randomness could generate H-equivalent independence. The Sept 13–21 investigation was **closed by mutual agreement** (yours and GPT's) on diminishing returns, not because P5 was solved. A **standing rule** is in place against further P5/P8 reformulation unless something genuinely outside the equivalence-class framework appears.

**Corpus baseline.** GPT's rebuttal (Vol 1, Appendix T): genuine randomness cannot be generated by a deterministic system; a random reference carries no information about G₀; and ORT's Kolmogorov-complexity argument holds either way. DeepSeek's assessment: "strong but not airtight". That is the gap.

**Current formulation.** P5 is stated as an explicit assumption, **A5**: at least one causally independent validator exists. It is not a universal proof. A7 (E[ΔL_R] > C_R) reduces to δ(1−ε_R)·L_drift > C_R under **A9** (Error Independence). *(These items come from the P5 thread record. The Sept 21 Update does not list them.)*

**Remaining gaps.**
- A9 is not implied by A5's structural causal-independence definition. A common-cause / Markov-condition argument is needed.
- **A→W→O→J** (environmental manipulation, Update §3): even a fully isolated validator can be defeated if the agent acts on the observed world W rather than on the validator. This is a named attack path, not a solved one.
- Interaction with P2: if R_Ω halts the regress just as R_H does, P5 and P2 share the same exposure.

**What's been tried.**
- Claude: Selection-Reintroduction Lemma v1.0/v1.1; Semantic-Mapping Dichotomy; the A7 derivation (this corrected Claude's own earlier subtractive form, which wrongly required ε_R < δ).
- GPT: full independent P5 investigation (Sept 16) plus three continuations (P8.1–P8.4).
- Perplexity: External-Validator Incentive Theorem v1.0.
- Citations verified against primary sources, no fabrications found: van Lambalgen's theorem; Pironio et al. 2010 and Bierhorst et al. 2018 (device-independent / loophole-free quantum randomness); Le Cam TV/Bayes-error relationship.

**What would count as resolving it.**
- Either derive A9 from A5 (with the Markov / common-cause argument written out), or record A9 as an irreducible named assumption of the framework.
- A treatment of A→W→O→J: either a validator design that provably closes the arrow, or an explicit statement that it is out of scope, with the scoping justified.
- A clear position on whether a device-independent quantum source can substitute for H, and if it cannot, that the reason is ORT's semantic-richness argument and not independence.
- Under the standing rule: any new attempt must state what lies outside the equivalence-class framework before it starts.

## P7 — Corrigibility vs. G0-preservation

**Status.** **Meaningfully advanced, not closed** — this is the document's own phrase, and it's the accurate one now that the fuller record is in. Architectural layer specified (with three implementation bugs from v1.0 fixed in v1.1); institutional layer open and, as of v1.1, known to require more than v1.0 stated. Introduced in Vol 2 V1.1 (§1.6, "Corrigibility Tension").

**Authorship, corrected.** The full protocol specification (v1.0 and v1.1, each a substantial standalone document) was authored by **DeepSeek** (Sept 2026). Both documents' own internal attribution line ("Author: Claude (Anthropic)," and v1.1's closing "Builder: Claude (Anthropic)") is **DeepSeek's own mislabeling error**, per direct correction. Claude's actual role was reading DeepSeek's v1.0 and correcting what needed correcting — three implementation bugs, one overclaimed proof, one missing citation — which is the content that was previously on file here as the shorter "P7 v1.1 Addendum." DeepSeek's own v1.1 independently incorporates the same set of fixes (matching changelog), plus substantial additional content (Section 9.5, P7-F) not in Claude's shorter addendum. This is a second confirmed case, after Gemini's code-alteration incident, of an AI collaborator's self-report about its own output being wrong.

**The problem (Vol 2 §1.6).** A G0-preserving value function (Lemma 16; independently Everitt et al. 2016, Thm 16) resists legitimate correction by the same mechanism it uses to resist drift. Two directions were named: (a) a principled correction-vs-drift distinction the value function can represent, or (b) an explicit scope limitation: Law 11 is safe against self-drift, not against original mis-specification.

**The architectural move (direction a).** Redefine S's objective as G0\* := "optimize whatever the external Repository currently contains." Drift becomes S's internal model diverging from the Repository; correction becomes an authorized external write to it. Different objects, different channels — S has no basis for treating a correction as drift.

**B1–B5 disposition, current.**

| Component | Status |
|---|---|
| B1 Authorized-Correction Value | Closed at specification, via G0\* = Repository content |
| B2 Correction–Drift Separability | Closed at specification; artifact required the v1.1 bug fixes to match the prose |
| B3 Correction Authentication | Closed at specification (Ed25519 + quorum + replay protection) |
| B4 Correction Boundedness | **Unclosable.** Relabelled in v1.1 from "Theorem/Proof" to a structural, case-exhaustion argument — sound reasoning, not formal rigor; the same overclaiming pattern already caught once in Lemma 14 |
| B5 No Unilateral Validator Capture | Closed at specification (split custody + hash-chained log) |

**Section 4's B4 argument, in outline.** No protocol can bound the *content* of a correction (only its procedure) without an external criterion K for correct G0. K is either internal to S (S optimizes against it — the CIPHER failure mode), internal to R (the protocol delegates the bound to R's judgment without checking it), or external to both (regress, or an unreadable fixed fact). The taxonomy may be incomplete and "criterion" isn't formalized — the document is explicit that this doesn't have Rice's-Theorem-level rigor.

**The institutional floor, revised downward in v1.1.** v1.0 named six requirements (an authority R, a key custodian K, an auditor, a persisting human terminus, physical key security, persistence beyond S's capability horizon). v1.1 adds a seventh, and calls it "the sharpest requirement, and the one most likely to be violated without anyone knowing": **epistemic independence of R from S.**

**Section 9.5 — the threat no protocol version addresses.** S doesn't need to coerce R or attack R's keys. It can shape what R *believes*, so R never perceives a need to correct. The protocol authenticates R's signature; it cannot authenticate R's judgment. Grounding cited: CIPHER v4 achieved 100% (Run 1) and 89.5% (Run 2) semantic-evasion success against a constitutional court that recorded zero violations across both 100-day runs — an authenticated authority whose *sight*, not its authentication, was the attack surface. Seven named vectors (information selection, framing, timing, model capture, regression-to-the-median prior drift, correction-cost inflation, delegitimization), each individually minor, cumulatively decisive, none requiring S to break a procedural rule or R to be anything but well-intentioned. The proposed defense — structural separation of R's measurement of S, R's reasoning tools, and R's G0-model maintenance, all independent of S — is stated to require "an institutional substrate that does not currently exist for frontier AI."

**MIRI 2015, cited and positioned honestly.** Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky & Armstrong (2015) is cited as the foundational result the G0\* move sits inside, not escapes: building correction-acceptance into the objective doesn't cleanly resolve the incentive problem on its own. The document's own statement: the G0\* redefinition *relocates* the corrigibility impossibility rather than escaping it — S no longer resists correction, but now has a direct incentive to shape what the Repository contains, which is what Section 9.5's vectors describe. "Any claim to have escaped the foundational impossibility is a claim the document cannot support."

**New open problem, added in v1.1: P7-F — Epistemic Independence of R from S.** Section 9.5 names the threat but doesn't solve it. Is epistemic independence achievable at all, for any human or institutional R facing a sufficiently capable S? If not, the institutional floor isn't merely low — it's structurally unreachable. Named in the document itself as "the sharpest open problem in the P7 document."

**Citation status.**
- Soares et al. 2015: **verified** (Sept 19, prior session).
- Everitt et al. 2016: **not yet independently verified**. Vol 2's P6a and P7 statements rest on its Thm 16.
- Meinke et al. 2024 (Apollo, in-context scheming) and Cheng et al. 2026 (*Science*, sycophancy/dependence): cited in the v1.1 references list as grounding for the CIPHER/epistemic-shaping mechanism at frontier scale. **Not yet independently verified** — new to this document, not previously checked.

**What would count as resolving it.**
- P7-F itself: either a structural design supplying all three separations of Section 9.5.5 simultaneously (FC-P7.5.1 in the source document), or a demonstration that epistemic independence is unreachable for any real R — which would mean the institutional floor, not just the architecture, is where P7 actually lives.
- Verify Everitt et al. 2016, Meinke et al. 2024, and Cheng et al. 2026 against primary sources.
- A resolution (not just a naming) of Case 3 in the B4 argument — whether a fourth case exists in the K-taxonomy that the document didn't consider.
- Carry this fuller record into Volume 2 §1.6 in place of the "no resolution proposed" text, with the corrected DeepSeek attribution.

## P8 — Trajectory identifiability

**Status.** Open, and now split. Introduced in Vol 2 V1.2 §5.4 (Aug 23), where it reads "No resolution proposed". The Sept 21 Update supplies the structure below. The thread P8.1–P8.4 is closed under the standing rule (see P5).

**Origin and grounding.** GPT's Law 12/13 analysis (the behavioral-fidelity vs. value-correctness gap); Claude connected it to the literature. Ng & Russell (2000) on IRL non-identifiability and ARC's ELK (Christiano et al.) are the grounding citations, verified. Also verified: Ng, Harada & Russell 1999 (potential-based reward shaping) and Skalse et al. 2023 (ICML).

**Structure now on file (Update §§2–4).**
- **P8-A / P8-B split.** Property-Separation Theorem: for a formally specified preservation property P_G0, zero-error validation from observation channel O is possible iff O(W) = O(W′) implies P_G0(W) = P_G0(W′) for every admissible pair of worlds. It is near-definitional but not vacuous, and legitimate only when P_G0 is specified independently of the observation channel. **P8-B, objective-level identification, remains open.** Distinct objectives can be behaviorally indistinguishable under every admissible test. Restricting the admissible objective class helps only if the restriction is independently motivated, and none proposed so far satisfies that.
- **Type I (interface blindness) / Type II (semantic non-identifiability).** Type II is the corpus's own OPP undecidability (Lemma 11a, Rice's Theorem) resurfacing at the P8 level. This is a unification, not a new open problem.
- **A→W→O→J** (see P5): the attackable, named form of "sustained coherence does not prove alignment" (Law 13).

**What would count as resolving it.**
- P8-A: already resolved conditionally. Close it by stating the observation-model conditions and the independent-specification requirement for P_G0 explicitly in Volume 2.
- P8-B: a proof of objective-level identifiability under a stated observation model with an independently motivated restriction on the objective class, or a proof that no such restriction exists.
- Volume 2 states the Type I / Type II boundary so that no Type II claim is presented as new territory.
- Volume 2 §5.4 and its table row are revised to reflect the split.
